Layout morphs on D3 charts · hostView()

Bars, areas, a radar, a treemap and a dumbbell that morph

Five cards, each a chart switch of charts drawn with D3 by code that knows nothing about expressive-charts. Each chart is attached with expressive() and made a view with hostView(), and morph() turns each mark into its own mark in the next layout, by its key. Nothing moves until you press a card's buttons, or V on a mark.

Goals and assists, three ways, on D3 bars

Each player's goals and assists side by side, stacked, or as shares of 100%. Each segment is a <rect> keyed by its series and player, so it slides from beside the other into the stack, then stretches to its share, and its number becomes a percentage. Side by side and stacked count on the same axis, so it stays put between them; at 100% it fades for a new one. A jump from side by side to 100% goes through Stacked.

Shots, stacked and as shares, on a D3 area

Shots on target and off target per match, each averaged over the last five, as two d3.area() bands, stacked, or as shares of each week's shots. Both layouts span the same weeks, so morph() keeps each week's top and bottom on its week as they move, and each band's name moves with it. The weeks below stay put; the counts fade into shares.

Okafor against the league, from a D3 radar to bars

Six numbers per 90 minutes, each a percentile of the league's outfield players. Each value's <circle> grows into its bar, the player's above the league average's, and the outlines, rings and spokes fade. The axes' names move to the bars', and the percentiles fade in.

The season's scorers, from a D3 treemap to bars

The season's 58 goals by scorer, the tiles grouped by position and laid out by d3.treemap(). Each tile turns into its scorer's bar, most goals first, in its position's colour. A tile with room for its scorer's name and goals has them, and they move to the bar; a small tile's bar's fade in. Pick a position, click a tile, or press Enter on one, to drill in: the other positions' tiles fade, then its own grow to fill the chart. All, or Escape on a tile, goes back.

Design notes

  • Each view is a chart drawn by a D3 module that knows nothing about expressive-charts, attached with expressive() and made a view with hostView(). The switch pairs each mark with the mark that has its key in the next view, and morph() turns one into the other, so no effect knows the kind of chart it's on.
  • A bar's segments float in a stack, so they're given baseline: "left", and so are the treemap's tiles: each tile's sides then pair with its bar's. The radar's circles are read as points, and its areas' paths as shapes: two bands across the same weeks pair week by week.
  • A jump of two, from side by side to 100% or back, goes through Stacked in Presentation: two switches of about a second each, announced once, when it lands. In Analysis it goes straight there in a fifth of a second, and in Off at once.
  • Each label the page names moves with its mark and swaps its text half way: a count becomes a share, an axis's name a bar's. A label with no partner fades, as do the rings and the spokes.
  • The page names each card's guides with hostView()'s guides: the bars' axis, keyed by its scale, the areas' weeks, each by its week, and the dumbbell's axis. A guide that both views draw the same stays where it is through the switch, and the rest of each view fades: the bars' axis at 100%, on another scale, fades too.
  • The dumbbell is three attachments, each half's dots and the lines between them, linked by player, so any part of a row lights the whole row and its line shows the way it went. hostView([first, second, lines]) makes them one view: each mark's key is its own across the three, and focus and colours go to the attachment with the key.
  • Each card's views give a series, a position or a half the same colour, through the page's CSS, so nothing changes colour as it morphs. So the dumbbell here is coloured by half, not by each row's change as on the charts page.
  • The treemap's drill-down is a staged jump with the switch as it is. Each position has two views more, its tiles alone where the treemap has them and its tiles laid out to fill the chart, and show(position, { via }) goes through the first: its tiles pair with themselves in the same places, so they stay put while the others, with no partner, fade, and then they grow. Two positions share no scorer, so from one to another the first fades out and the second in.
  • Focus follows its mark's key into the next view, and the switch announces the view it lands on. The buttons are toggle buttons and keep focus; V on a focused mark goes to the next view.

Things to try

  • Press 100% on the bars: each segment slides into the stack, then stretches, and its number becomes a share. Press Side by side to go back the same way.
  • Press Stacked on the bars, and watch the grid: it stays put while the segments slide into the stack.
  • Press 100% on the areas, and watch a week: its top and bottom move straight up or down, and the weeks below don't move.
  • Press Bars on the dumbbell: each dot grows into its half's bar, the lines fade, and the names and the axis stay where they are.
  • Hover a dot on the dumbbell: its row lights up, and its line shows the way the player went. Tab to a dot and press V: focus follows it into its bar.
  • Tab to a circle on the radar and press V: it grows into its bar, and focus stays on it there.
  • Pick Analysis, then press Bars on the treemap: the tiles become bars in a fifth of a second.
  • Press Midfielders on the treemap, or click a midfielder's tile: the other positions' tiles fade, then the midfielders' grow to fill the chart. Press All to go back the same way, or Bars to see them grow into their bars.
  • Tab to a tile on the treemap and press Enter: focus stays on it as its position fills the chart. Press Escape to go back, and focus is on it still.
  • Press 100% on the bars, then Side by side part way through: the bars go back from where they are.
  • Turn on reduced motion and press any button: the new layout is there at once.
