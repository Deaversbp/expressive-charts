Four charts drawn with D3 · expressive()

A waterfall, a dumbbell, a radar and a butterfly

Four charts from the ideas doc, each drawn with D3 by code that knows nothing about expressive-charts, and given its effects through expressive(). Where a chart's marks aren't what the adapter reads from their tags, the page describes them with describe: the waterfall's floating steps, and the radar's outlines and values. The waterfall and the butterfly play at load; the dumbbell and the radar play when you hover or Tab into them.

Goal difference, on a D3 waterfall

Harbor City's goal difference month by month, and the season's total. Each step is a <rect> described with describe.bar and its own baseline, and keeps its signed value (label). At load the connectors draw in (drawIn) and a ball travels down the staircase to rest on the total. Press Replay to see it again, or Tab to the steps.

First half against second, on a D3 dumbbell

Each player's goals before and after the turn of the year: a <line> from a hollow dot to a filled one, sorted by the change. Hover a dot or a line, or Tab to them: an arrow travels from the first half to the second, so the way it goes is the change. Switch to Assists to see the dots move, then the rows re-sort.

Okafor against the league, on a D3 radar

Six numbers per 90 minutes, each a percentile of the league's outfield players. The outlines are <polygon>s described as lines, so the player's is coloured where it's past the league average (threshold). Hover a value, or Tab to the values: a dot travels out its spoke, and the same axis lights on the other shape.

When goals come, on a D3 butterfly

The season's goals in each 15 minutes of a match, conceded on the left and scored on the right. Each half is an attachment of its own on one <svg>, so each grows out from the middle at load (drawIn), and the two are linked, so hovering a bar lights its mirror. Press Replay to see them grow again.

Design notes

  • Each chart is drawn by a module that knows nothing about expressive-charts, as a D3 author's would be. What the adapter can't read from an element's tag, the page describes, so the effects stay blind to the kind of chart they're on.
  • A waterfall's steps float: no edge is shared by every step, so the page gives each its own baseline with describe.bar, up from its bottom for a rise and down from its top for a fall.
  • The staircase is a path of its own, with no keyboard or names: the steps' names already say each month and the running total. The ball rests on the total, the total's label above it.
  • A closed shape has no direction, so the radar's outlines are described as lines from the first corner round to the first again. Their samples are each outline's distance from the centre on shared rays, so the colour ends where the outlines cross.
  • A link lights the marks in its other attachments, so the dumbbell's lines and each half's dots are three attachments linked by player: any part of a row lights the whole row. Only the lines take the keyboard, each naming both halves, so a row is read once.
  • The dumbbell's change is staged as a bar chart's is: the values first, then the rows, in Presentation; both at once in Analysis; at once in Off. Its own transition reads the motion mode, and watch keeps the effects on the marks as they move.
  • A linked hover lights the other marks and shows nothing else: no tooltip, and nothing announced.

Things to try

  • Press the waterfall's Replay: the connectors draw in and the ball travels down the staircase to rest on the total.
  • Hover Okafor's line on the dumbbell: the arrow goes left, since he scored fewer in the second half. Then switch to Assists while hovering.
  • Pick Analysis, then switch the dumbbell back to Goals: the dots and the rows move together, in a fifth of a second.
  • Hover a value on the radar, or Tab to it and use the arrow keys: a dot travels out its spoke, and the league average lights on the same axis.
  • Turn on reduced motion and reload: the ball is on the total at once, the labels are in place, and the halves are drawn.
